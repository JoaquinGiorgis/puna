# Modelos de negocio de vendors de software para aseguradoras (insurtech B2B SaaS), 2023–2026, incl. LatAm

Scope: vendors that sell tools to insurers (no risk-taking, no MGA). Research done 2026-11-12 with ~17 search/fetch calls; many figures come from search-result snippets of press coverage. Where only an aggregator or vendor blog was found, it is flagged. Items marked (ESTIMATE) are analyst/blog figures, not disclosed by the company.

## 1. Companies and traction (ARR, customers, funding, valuations, exits)

### Takeaway
The category has produced real scale and exits: one public core-systems leader (Guidewire, >US$1B ARR) and three big take-privates or strategic acquisitions in 2023–2025 (Duck Creek US$2.6B, Sapiens US$2.5B, CAPE Analytics to Moody's). AI point solutions (Shift, Tractable, Akur8, Gradient AI) raised large rounds, but most hard revenue numbers are undisclosed.

### Cited Findings

Total revenue US$1,202.5M (+23%). 57 core cloud deals in the year, 19 in Q4. — [Guidewire FY2025 results, Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250904110162/en); [Guidewire IR](https://ir.guidewire.com/node/24861)

- Duck Creek: Vista Equity Partners agreed in Jan 2023 to buy it for about US$2.6B (US$19/share in cash, a 64% premium to the 30-day VWAP). — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/duck-creek-agrees-to-be-acquired-by-vista-equity-partners-for-2-6-billion/); [Vista press release](https://www.vistaequitypartners.com/news/duck-creek-agrees-to-be-acquired-by-vista-equity-partners-for-2-6-billion/)

### Inferences
- Exit multiples for insurance software are moderate. Sapiens sold at about 4.6x revenue (derived), and Duck Creek and Sapiens both went to PE. The money is in sticky, recurring, multi-year contracts, not hypergrowth.
- CAPE → Moody's is the most relevant precedent for PUNA. A geospatial/satellite data-and-verification tool ended up inside a risk-model and data incumbent. Moody's, Verisk, Swiss Re/Munich Re-type players are natural partners or acquirers.
- In Argentine agro, the incumbent pattern is "data provider (public CONAE data or an agtech) feeds the insurer's index product". The insurer keeps the risk and the customer relationship, and the tech provider stays behind the scenes.

### Gaps
- Shift, Tractable, Akur8, Hi Marley, Sprout.ai and Gradient AI ARR: not publicly disclosed, and no credible estimate was found.
- Earnix and FRISS 2023–2026 funding and customers: searches returned nothing usable.
- Guidewire total customer count: not retrieved (it is in the 10-K).
- Argentine B2B insurance SaaS beyond Bdeo: the brief's candidates (Sima, Sirena, Wenance) could not be verified as insurer-facing SaaS. Wenance is a lending fintech, and Sirena is a WhatsApp CRM (both from prior knowledge, not verified this session). Whether Kilimo, Auravant or Agrotoken sell to insurers was also not verified. No Finnovista Insurtech Radar Argentina data was retrieved.

## 2. Pricing units (per claim, per policy, % GWP/DWP, licence, implementation, outcome-based)

### Takeaway
Core systems price on premium volume: Guidewire explicitly prices as a % of direct written premium (DWP) under management, never per seat. Point solutions use per-transaction (per claim or per policy) or base-plus-usage pricing. Outcome-based pricing (share of savings) is discussed often but rarely documented with public numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Guidewire 10-K language: "We generally price our licenses, services and products based on the amount of direct written premiums ('DWP') that will be managed by our solutions." Management has said cloud subscriptions are recurring and priced as a % of DWP, and the company has never used seats. — [Guidewire 10-Q/10-K text via CapEdge](https://capedge.com/files/DIFF/10-Q/0001528396-18-000019_0001528396-23-000137.htm.gz); [Panabee FY25 summary](https://www.panabee.com/news/guidewire-software-earnings-2025-annual)
- Consequence: Guidewire's revenue is tied to its clients' premium growth, including inflation and catastrophe effects. — [Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/guidewire-software-earnings-2025-annual)
- (ESTIMATE) % of premium vendor pricing is about 0.5%–2% of premium processed, falling as volume grows. This is attributed to a 2022 Novarica report but seen only via a vendor-pricing blog, not the primary source. — [Monetizely procurement guide](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/procurement-guide-how-are-insurance-policy-administration-amp-claims-systems-priced-for-enterprises); [Monetizely, carriers SaaS metric](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/which-pricing-metric-fits-insurance-carriers-saas-best-per-seat-per-transaction-or-per-outcome)
- (ESTIMATE) Per-policy fees run about US$3–15 per policy per year. Illustrative transaction prices are "US$5 per policy issued, US$10 per claim processed". These are blog figures, not benchmarks. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/which-pricing-metric-fits-insurtech-mgas-saas-best-per-seat-per-transaction-or-per-outcome)
- (OPINION) For claims AI, success-based pricing (% of demonstrated savings) is described as most effective. Outcome metrics cited include % of loss-ratio improvement and a share of operating-cost savings. — [Monetizely AI underwriting pricing](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-determine-the-right-pricing-for-ai-underwriting-in-insurance); [Insurance Edge, "Outcomes as a Service", Dec 2025](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/12/11/the-quiet-shift-to-outcomes-as-a-service/)
- Hybrid (subscription plus usage/outcome) models in B2B SaaS generally rose from 27% to 41% (2025 State of B2B Monetization). This is not insurance-specific and is cited second-hand. — [Curacel](https://www.curacel.co/post/pay-for-outcomes-not-access-how-ai-is-rewriting-software-pricing)

### Inferences
- A defensible PUNA structure would be: (a) an annual platform fee per insurer, sized by agro premium or insured hectares; plus (b) a per-verified-claim or per-policy-in-force fee; with (c) implementation as a one-off. A pure % of claims paid would look like a "cut" and would tie PUNA's revenue to disasters (see §3).
- A per-hectare or per-policy fee maps naturally to how agro insurers already measure books (insured hectares, as in the 23.5M ha figure).

### Gaps
- No published price list or contract was found for Shift, Tractable, Akur8 or Bdeo. Real unit prices for per-claim AI vendors are not public.
- Implementation-fee ratios (e.g., Guidewire services revenue vs subscription) were not extracted.

## 3. How vendors avoid being "a partner taking a cut" (contracts, licensing, outcome pricing)

### Takeaway
Vendors stay out of the regulated intermediary or carrier perimeter by selling a licence or subscription to the insurer, not a share of premium tied to placing business. In Argentina, intermediation (promoting contracts and advising insureds) is reserved to licensed productores asesores under Ley 22.400, so a tool must not sell or advise on policies to farmers or earn commission-like fees. Premium collection is restricted to BCRA-registered PSPs.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 22.400 regulates "la actividad de intermediación en la concertación de contratos de seguros". Intermediation means promoting the conclusion of insurance contracts and advising insureds and prospective insureds. Only matriculated PAS registered with the SSN may operate. — [SSN, Sociedades de PAS](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/superintendencia-de-seguros/mercado-asegurador/sociedades-de-productores-asesores-de-seguros-pas-0); [SSN, PAS](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/57790)
- SSN Res. 406/2024 (22 Aug 2024), on premium collection systems:
  - Art. 1: insurers must notify the SSN within 5 business days of signing, changing or terminating premium-collection contracts, and keep them available to the SSN, which may object.
  - Art. 2: only entities in the BCRA Registro de Proveedores de Servicios de Pago may provide electronic collection and processing.
  - Art. 3: such PSPs may provide only the collection service to insurers.
  - Art. 8: 180-day adaptation period.
  - Scope is premium collection only, not IT or tech outsourcing in general, and not claim payouts.
  — [Res. SSN 406/2024 PDF](https://todoriesgo.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/406-BO.pdf)
- The consolidated RGAA (Reglamento General de la Actividad Aseguradora) text was approved by RESOL-2024-498-APN-SSN#MEC (01/10/2024). It may contain IT or outsourcing provisions, but they were not verified. — [RGAA consolidated](https://argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/ssn_241003_rgaa.pdf)
- Guidewire's DWP-based fee is a software price indexed to premium managed, not a commission on sales. It is framed as a licence/subscription price. — [Guidewire 10-K text via CapEdge](https://capedge.com/files/DIFF/10-Q/0001528396-18-000019_0001528396-23-000137.htm.gz)

### Inferences
- PUNA must be contracted by the insurer as a technology provider. The insurer issues the policy, keeps the risk and decides the payout, and PUNA supplies verification evidence and an automated settlement rail.
  - Fees must not depend on policies sold to farmers, which could look like intermediation. They also should not take a % of indemnities, which creates a perverse incentive and looks like a cut.
  - A % of DWP or a per-policy fee is the accepted industry pattern.
- Payout via an on-chain escrow: Res. 406/2024 covers premium collection, not claims payment, but the parallel suggests money movement for insurers must go through regulated rails. In the Argentine pitch, the escrow should be presented as the insurer's own disbursement instruction or a proof layer, with fiat settlement through the insurer's bank or PSP. This is a legal question to validate, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No specific SSN norm on IT outsourcing, cloud, or third-party tech/claims-adjusting providers was found. The RGAA consolidated text should be checked for "tercerización" and "sistemas".
- How Shift or Tractable structure outcome-based contracts in practice (caps, baselines, audit of savings) was not documented in public sources found.
- Insurer IT procurement norms in Argentina (RFP practice, security due diligence) were not found.

## 4. Sales cycles and pilot / PoC norms

### Takeaway
Expect about 12–18 months from first contact to a production contract with an insurer, preceded by weeks to months of security and compliance due diligence. The common entry point is a scoped 90–120 day PoC with pre-agreed success metrics, and pilots often stall around month 6 without an executive sponsor and a path to production.

### Cited Findings
- (OPINION, insurtech advisory) Production deals often take 12–18 months. Due diligence (regulatory, security, data) before a pilot can take weeks to months. Insurers prefer proof that a solution worked at competitors, and PoC (does the tech work) differs from proof of value (business benefit). — [Insurance Edge, Dec 2025](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/12/12/5-essentials-for-insurtechs-to-become-enterprise-ready/); [Clearspeed](https://clearspeed.com/blog/insurance-edge-5-essentials-for-insurtechs)
- (VENDOR OPINION) A structured 90–120 day PoC is called "the single most reliable way to convert carrier interest into a live contract". Running compliance review in parallel with sales is claimed to shorten cycles by about 30% (unverified). — [Hyperexponential blog 2026](https://www.hyperexponential.com/blog/insurance-technology-in-2026-how-ai-is-reshaping-vendor-carrier-relationships-for-insurance-organizations); [IB Applications](https://ibapplications.com/content-library/blog/tips-for-accelerating-insurance-go-to-market-in-2026)
- (SPONSORED) AI pilots typically launch with momentum and stall about 6 months later. — [Carrier Management / Cytora](https://www.carriermanagement.com/brand-spotlight/cytora/from-pilot-to-foundation-why-most-ai-projects-get-stuck/)
- An Alvarez & Marsal and InsurTech NY report (2026) found many insurers struggle to convert promising insurtech partnerships into enterprise value. — [Crowdfund Insider, Mar 2026](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/266346-insurtech-providers-under-pressure-to-turn-ai-pilots-to-tangle-business-outcomes-report-reveals/)
- Multi-year renewals are the norm with anchor clients, e.g., Shift–AXA renewed for 5 years. — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/five-year-renewal-of-collaboration-between-shift-technology-and-axa-to-accelerate-ai-powered-insurance-transformation/)
- Strategic investors drive distribution: Guidewire invested in Akur8, and Hollard and Telefónica Seguros (via Íope) invested in Bdeo. — [Akur8](https://www.akur8.com/news/akur8-secures-120-million-in-series-c-round-to-accelerate-growth-of-its-next-generation-actuarial-platform); [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/06/29/spanish-insurtech-startup-bdeo-scans-in-eur75-million/)

### Inferences
- For PUNA, a parametric agro pilot fits a crop campaign naturally. One season (e.g., a 2026/27 soy/maize campaign) on a shadow basis, run in parallel with traditional adjusters, would be the PoC, with success metrics agreed in advance:
  - agreement rate with adjusters;
  - time-to-pay;
  - cost per claim.
- Reference customers matter more than features. A first Argentine agro insurer, or a CONAE/INTA-style public data partner, de-risks later sales.
- Core-platform partnerships (as with Guidewire–Akur8) are a distribution path. In LatAm, the equivalent would be partnering with the policy-admin vendors used by Argentine insurers (not researched).

### Gaps
- No LatAm- or Argentina-specific data on insurer sales cycles or pilot norms.
- No independent (non-vendor) survey quantifying insurtech sales cycle length.
